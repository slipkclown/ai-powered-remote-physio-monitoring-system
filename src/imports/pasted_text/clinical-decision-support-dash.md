Update the **Recovery Insights Dashboard** to transform it from a patient monitoring dashboard into an intelligent **Clinical Decision Support Dashboard**. The overall goal is to help physiotherapists quickly understand not only what the patient's rehabilitation data is, but what it means, why it matters, and which patients require attention. Every section should guide decision making rather than simply displaying numbers.

Maintain the existing navigation, page structure, and overall visual identity, but redesign the information hierarchy so the dashboard tells a complete recovery story.

Replace the current "Recovery Score" card with a more meaningful **Recovery Status** card. Instead of displaying only a percentage, classify the patient's current recovery state using categories such as **On Track**, **Needs Attention**, or **High Risk**, accompanied by a short explanation summarising the patient's overall progress.

Introduce a new **Recovery Risk Level** card that analyses multiple recovery indicators simultaneously. Rather than relying on movement quality alone, calculate the overall recovery risk based on exercise performance, rehabilitation adherence, confidence level, pain score, motivation, journal reflections, and recovery trends. Display the risk level using colour coded indicators with a concise explanation of the primary contributing factors.

Add a prominent **AI Recovery Intelligence Summary** at the top of the dashboard. This section should automatically generate a concise clinical interpretation of the patient's overall rehabilitation progress. Instead of presenting raw data, provide a meaningful narrative explaining positive progress, areas of concern, behavioural patterns, emotional wellbeing observations, rehabilitation adherence, and possible reasons for changes in recovery. The summary should read like an intelligent clinical assistant supporting physiotherapists rather than replacing their professional judgement.

Introduce an **AI Suggested Actions** panel directly below the Recovery Intelligence Summary. Based on the interpreted recovery data, generate practical recommendations for physiotherapists such as reviewing exercise progression, discussing pain management strategies, encouraging patient confidence, scheduling an earlier follow up appointment, or maintaining the current rehabilitation programme. These recommendations should serve as clinical guidance rather than automated decisions.

Replace isolated numerical values with **trend based visualisations** wherever possible. For confidence, pain, motivation, adherence, and movement quality, display whether each indicator is improving, declining, or remaining stable over time using small trend graphs or directional indicators. Emphasise rehabilitation progression instead of single session measurements.

Transform the existing Recovery Compass into the central visual element of the dashboard. Rather than displaying independent cards only, create a holistic recovery overview that combines Physical Recovery, Rehabilitation Adherence, Confidence, Pain Management, Motivation, Behavioural Engagement, and Emotional Wellbeing into a single visual summary. This should become the primary representation of the patient's overall recovery status.

Enhance the Journal Review section by introducing intelligent reflection analysis. Instead of simply displaying journal entries, identify recurring themes such as fear of reinjury, frustration, confidence improvement, pain concerns, or positive milestones. Highlight these themes visually and connect them to the overall recovery assessment. Allow physiotherapists to quickly understand patient concerns without reading every journal entry in detail.

Create a dedicated **Priority Alerts** section that automatically identifies patients requiring immediate attention. Examples include declining confidence, reduced rehabilitation adherence, increasing pain levels, missed exercise sessions, unread patient reflections, or significant changes in recovery trends. Present these alerts using a clean priority system that allows physiotherapists to quickly identify the most urgent cases.

Introduce a chronological **Recovery Timeline** showing key rehabilitation milestones throughout the patient's recovery journey. Include completed rehabilitation stages, progress milestones, reported concerns, physiotherapist reviews, and important clinical events to provide context for the patient's current recovery status.

Where appropriate, replace raw percentages with meaningful descriptions and concise clinical interpretations. Every metric should answer not only "What is the value?" but also "Why is this important?" and "What should the physiotherapist pay attention to next?"

Maintain the current minimalist aesthetic using the established colour palette with **Deep Ocean Blue (#305066)** as the primary accent colour and **Cream (#FAF4E5)** as the primary background. Use generous white space, soft shadows, rounded cards, elegant typography, and a clean information hierarchy. Ensure the interface feels calm, professional, trustworthy, and approachable rather than resembling a traditional hospital information system.

The final dashboard should communicate that Recovr is not simply monitoring rehabilitation exercises. Instead, it should function as an intelligent rehabilitation companion that transforms movement data, behavioural indicators, and patient reported experiences into meaningful clinical intelligence that supports personalised physiotherapy and more informed rehabilitation decision making.
